Silicon in human biology

Silicon is present in the human body and is associated with collagen-rich connective tissues, including bone and cartilage. It is also found in skin, hair and nails.

Research describes a structural relationship between silicon and collagen, as well as the wider extracellular matrix that gives connective tissues their framework. Human nutrition studies have reported lower average dietary silicon intake in women than in men. Silicon remains a long-overlooked element in human biology and deserves attention in women’s nutrition research.

Bioavailability makes the difference.

The nutritional value of silicon depends not only on the amount present, but especially on its bioavailability.

SilaRex Bioavailable Silicon Technology is designed to maintain silicon in its biologically available form throughout the product’s shelf life, alongside selected micronutrients.

Take 10 drops (0.5 ml) daily, mixed into a quarter glass (50 to 70 ml) of water or fruit juice. Stir gently and drink immediately, preferably before breakfast. Shake the bottle first and do not take it undiluted.

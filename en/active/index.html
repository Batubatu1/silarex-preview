Silicon in human biology

Silicon is present in the human body and is associated with collagen-rich connective tissues, including bone and cartilage. It is also found in skin, hair and nails. Research describes a structural relationship between silicon and collagen, as well as the wider extracellular matrix that provides connective tissues with their framework.

Human studies have examined silicon in relation to bone, cartilage, skin, hair and nails. Some studies have reported positive findings, although the evidence varies by tissue and outcome. Research has also suggested that silicon concentrations may decrease with age in certain tissues. Whether this occurs throughout the body, or contributes to common signs of ageing, remains uncertain.

Silicon remains a long-overlooked element in human biology and deserves attention in human nutrition research.

Bioavailability makes the difference.

The nutritional value of silicon depends not only on the amount present, but especially on its bioavailability.
